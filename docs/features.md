# ServiceForce feature inventory

## Finding demo accounts and usernames

Start the local server and open [http://127.0.0.1:8000/](http://127.0.0.1:8000/). Use the demo workspace buttons for Administrator, Technician or Employee. All three use the demo password `RelayDemo2026!`. Each installation generates different usernames; **My profile** shows the signed-in user's assigned username in `nameCODE@service.role` format. The code has four letters or digits. Real account passwords are stored as hashes and cannot be read from Git.

## Accounts and access

- Employee registration requires an eight-digit code sent to the new email address. The address becomes the verified recovery email. The employee enters the workspace immediately after verification; administrator-created accounts still require their password to sign in.
- A role selection page leads to distinct Employee, Technician and Administrator sign-in screens with role-specific illustrations and motion. The selected role is checked during sign-in.
- The system assigns each account a name-based, role-based username such as `jordanleeA1B2@service.emp`, `samriveraC3D4@service.tech` or `alexmorganE5F6@service.adm`. The name is lowercased and spaces and punctuation are removed. The four-character code is generated by the system and cannot be chosen by users or administrators. Existing eight-character codes are shortened when possible; collisions receive a new code. Profile name and role changes update the username while preserving its four-character code. Each person sees their own username in My profile; administrator People lists omit it.
- Sign-in accepts only the assigned username and password. Users can request their username or reset their password through their verified recovery email. Email codes expire after 10 minutes, allow five attempts, and can be requested again after one minute.
- The forgot password screen starts with an empty recovery email field. My profile states that the assigned username is required for future sign-ins.
- When an open incident misses its original resolution deadline, ServiceForce keeps that breach visible, sets a priority-based recovery deadline (15–120 minutes), and adds up to two available technicians with the same primary skill as the incident category. These skill categories define the response teams. It prefers lower weighted workload and does not interrupt technicians handling work of equal or higher priority. The lead technician remains assigned, and every addition is recorded in the timeline. If no suitable teammate has capacity, administrators are alerted. Added responders can read the full incident history and attachments, receive updates, and work the incident. This first stage happens once per incident breach, even when the SLA monitor runs repeatedly. An administrator can add or remove teammates manually, with a limit of three additional responders and audit entries for each change.
- If the recovery deadline is also missed, ServiceForce marks the incident **Highly Critical** and alerts administrators once. The original resolution deadline is never replaced by the recovery deadline. Reopening an incident starts a fresh escalation cycle while retaining the prior audit history.
- Password-based sign-in and sign-out with sessions. Demo accounts are available in local demo mode.
- Employee, technician and administrator roles. Employees see their own incidents, technicians see assignments, and administrators see the full queue and settings.
- Administrator management of people, roles, technician skills, availability and workload capacity.
- The People page shows each technician's team (their primary skill), availability, and active workload. The administrator's Teams page groups technicians by skill, shows team membership and open incident counts, and links to each member's cases. General support is shown separately and is not automatically selected for category-specific escalation. Administrators move a technician to another team by editing their primary skill in People.
- Every signed-in person can edit their own name, recovery email and (for technicians) primary skill after confirming their current password. Changing the recovery email requires verification of the new address. Changing a password requires both the current password and a code sent to the verified recovery email.
- Administrators can remove accounts. Removed people lose access, while incident history remains available; active technician cases must be reassigned first.
- Administrators can open a technician's assigned incident list from the People page, including open and completed cases.

## Incident work

- Report incidents with a title, description, affected service, category, business impact and urgency.
- Automatic P1–P4 priority calculation from impact, urgency and service criticality.
- Automatic technician assignment using either skill-first or least-workload routing, subject to availability and capacity. Administrators can reassign incidents.
- Lifecycle: classified, assigned, acknowledged, in progress, resolved, verified and closed, with role-checked transitions.
- Edit open reports, add notes, request information, record a resolution and leave reporter feedback.
- Attach PNG, JPEG, PDF or text files (up to 1 MiB each) to an incident. Only people who can view that incident can download its files.
- Reporters or administrators can reopen resolved, verified or closed incidents with a reason. The incident returns to active work and gets a new resolution deadline.
- IT staff can publish searchable knowledge articles for services; employees can read the articles before reporting an issue.
- Public notes are visible to incident participants. Internal notes are available only to IT staff and are hidden from employee history and notifications.
- Assigned technicians and administrators can record root cause, impact and prevention steps in a post-incident review for resolved P1 incidents.
- Find similar incidents on the same service and suggest a previous resolution when available.
- Search and filter the incident queue by priority, status, category, service, technician, SLA, date and lifecycle.

## Service management and oversight

- Administrator management of service catalog, incident categories, routing strategy and response/resolution SLA policies.
- Response and resolution deadlines, at-risk and breached indicators, and periodic monitoring every 15 seconds while the server runs.
- **P1 response escalation:** when a critical incident passes its response deadline without acknowledgment, ServiceForce creates one escalation event, alerts administrators in-app, records it in the audit log and marks the incident Escalated in the queue and detail view. The marker records that escalation occurred, even if the incident is acknowledged later. Administrators can inspect and reassign the case.
- In-app notifications for incident activity and SLA events. Verified users also receive email updates through a persistent retrying outbox when Brevo is configured.
- Incident history and administrator audit log, operational overview, service volume, technician workload and SLA violation reports.
